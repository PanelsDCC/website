Future Features

A foundation, not a dead end

PanelsD.CC v1 focuses on clarity, accessibility, and enjoyment — establishing a solid operational foundation that's ready to grow.

What PanelsD.CC Doesn't Do (Yet)

And why that's intentional

PanelsD.CC v1 does not currently support sensors, live train position tracking, interlocking, automatic running, or fully automated route setting.

These capabilities are very much planned — and, importantly, the system has been built with them in mind from the start. This is most visible in how panels are designed: track sections already exist as first-class elements, with clearly defined areas where train presence and movement will be shown in future versions.

Nothing about the current architecture prevents these features. On the contrary, Panels has been deliberately structured so that sensor data, occupancy detection, and automation logic can be introduced without having to redesign panels, trains, or accessories later.

Why v1 Focuses on Fun

Lowering friction, raising enjoyment

The initial focus of PanelsD.CC has been to put the fun back into railway control.

As modern DCC capabilities — particularly sound and advanced decoder features — have become the norm, controlling them has often become harder, not easier. Many systems still rely on long function lists, memorised numbers, or constantly referring to manuals.

PanelsD.CC takes a different approach:

  • Functions are visual, not numeric
  • Sounds look like sounds
  • Lights look like lights
  • Operation is immediate and intuitive

Panels was designed so that my son, aged three, can operate trains, blow horns and whistles, and enjoy the railway without frustration. Just as importantly, it was designed so I can do the same — without mental overhead, lists, or remembering what F17 does on a particular locomotive.

A Foundation, Not a Dead End

By focusing first on clarity, accessibility, and enjoyment, PanelsD.CC establishes a solid operational foundation.

As sensors, interlocking, automation, and live train tracking are introduced, they will build on:

  • Visual panels that already understand layout structure
  • Accessories and signals that already behave realistically
  • A multi-device system designed for shared operation

The result is a system that starts simple, stays fun, and is ready to grow — without ever losing sight of why most of us got into the hobby in the first place.
